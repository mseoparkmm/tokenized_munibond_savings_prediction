Data, model & limitations

Model basis and limitations

Traditional bond · corrected positive-fee production model

436 Texas GO issues, six predicted components: UW, Bond Counsel, Financial Advisor, Paying Agent, Rating Agency Fee and Other. Underwriter and Paying Agent are assumed replaced for the illustrative tokenized comparison; the other four remain.

Nested expanding-window FY2022–2025: n=187 · MAE $71,120 · RMSE $121,965 · R² 0.840 · MAPE 18.38%.
Tokenized bond · full-factorial structural phase model (2026-11-09)

Hardhat-calibrated, separate USDC/RLUSD models of DEPLOY, SETUP, INVEST, TOKEN_DIST, PROCEEDS, COUPON and PRINCIPAL using N, ceil(N/B) and CPY×Maturity. Exact matching observed scenarios use recorded Gas Used; others use modeled Gas. Base L1 uses per-transaction fee-size estimates.

112,000 scenario grid; 13,010 directly measured; 98,990 model-predicted. Calibration: $10M par, 5% coupon; N 100–10,000, maturity 1–20 years; network pricing from 365 historical paired observations. Values outside experimental bounds are extrapolations.
Cost comparability limitation: Traditional model estimates issuance-related fees, while blockchain cost includes issuance and recurring lifecycle transactions. Conventional recurring servicing costs and some possible tokenization-related legal, platform, compliance and custody fees are not included. Reported “savings” are scenario estimates under these replacement assumptions, not verified net savings.

How the models evolved

From raw records and gas measurements to reproducible predictors.

Traditional model development

1
Build a consistent issuance sample

479 source issues → exclude 9 private placements, 31 issues with nonpositive component fees and 3 missing ratings → 436 final observations.

2
Choose predictors by fee mechanism

Use transaction size, credit rating, sale type and relevant issuer/transaction characteristics. Screen candidate variables separately for each service fee.

3
Validate chronologically; refit for deployment

Use nested expanding windows to assess forward prediction, then refit the selected estimators on all 436 issues.

Tokenized model development

1
Implement transaction lifecycle

Deploy and execute EVM bond contracts with USDC/RLUSD settlement; measure Economic Gas Used and transaction counts by phase.

2
Measure representative scenarios

Initial Step 8 included 210 successful scenario runs; build the first 365-day cost estimates for Ethereum, Base and XRPL EVM.

3
Expand to a 112,000-case factorial design

Vary investor count, batch size, coupon frequency, maturity and stablecoin. As of this interim model, 13,010 cases are directly measured.

4
Fit seven phase-specific Gas equations

Weighted linear models use investor count, batch count and payment events; Base gets an additional validated fee-data-size model.

Gas prediction logic

H = ceil(N / B)  ·  K = CPY × MY
H is the number of distribution batches; K is the total number of coupon events.
G = Σ phase Gas(N, H, K, stablecoin)
Deploy, Setup, Invest, Token Distribution, Proceeds, Coupon, Principal. First and subsequent coupon events are modeled separately.
Tx = 12 + 2N + (K+2)H + 2K
Deterministic economic transaction count in the current experiment design.

Ethereum MainnetG × daily ETH/USD-per-Gas
XRPL EVMG × daily XRP/USD-per-Gas
Base (L2 + L1)G × daily L2 rate + D × daily L1 rate

What are P10 / P50 / P90?

For each of 365 dates (Oct 26, 2025–Oct 25, 2026), the same-date network fee and asset price are combined into USD cost. The percentiles are then taken over these 365 total lifecycle-cost scenarios—not over Gas Price and asset price separately. D is Base's modeled L1 fee-data size.

Validation boundaries

Traditional test error is in USD issuance fees; tokenized errors are relative to simulated total Gas; Base error refers to estimated fee-data size. The RLUSD factorial sample is especially limited. Held-out and external sets supported model development but do not constitute live-network validation or an independent blind evaluation of every extreme configuration.

05

What is measured, predicted or assumed?

A necessary distinction when interpreting results or presenting them to decision-makers.

MEASUREDObserved inputs

436 actual issuance records; 13,010 directly measured Full Factorial Hardhat Gas cases; 365 historical daily network prices and exchange rates. The past fee data describe market conditions, not fees paid during the simulations.

PREDICTEDModel-derived outputs

Six traditional fee components; 98,990 Full Factorial Gas cases; Base L1 fee-data size; USD lifecycle costs and investor-count extrapolations beyond the 10,000-investor experimental range; maturity scenarios are currently supported through 30 years.

ASSUMEDComparison scenario

Underwriter and Paying Agent fees are fully replaced; four other issuance fees are retained. The tokenized Gas model is calibrated at $10M par, 5% coupon and $10 token price.

Scope limitation · Read before interpreting the estimated savings

The Traditional model predicts selected issuance fees; the blockchain model covers initial transactions plus coupon/principal servicing through maturity. Conventional ongoing servicing costs and some tokenization-specific legal, platform, custody and compliance costs are not fully represented. Consequently, the displayed difference is a conditional scenario estimate—not a fully matched lifecycle cost comparison or demonstrated net savings. Gas predictions outside 10,000 investors or 20 years are unvalidated extrapolations.
